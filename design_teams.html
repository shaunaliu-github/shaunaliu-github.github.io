Design Teams

I joined UTAT Space Systems, where I learned that complex projects have to be built up, one simple step at a time, one simple test at a time.

A Painful Debugging Experience

I learned the hard way that building complex software requires us to build small components of the code, test them, and only when the components work should we add more. At the beginning of the year, I started built a flowgraph that converted a high frequency radio signal to a digital signal. I watched a tutorial, and added everything they had to my graph. I had trust that their flowgraph would work for my purposes, even though I didn't understand their flow graph fully. I was wrong -- the flowgraph didn't work, and I have no idea why. My design team lead then instructed me to only build small components, and only when they work, build bigger components. This may seem really obvious, but I often skip steps to get to a final product quicker, which often leads to an extremely painful debugging experience.

I would like my future self, who will be using smarter AI to code, to remember this painful lesson: I should use only simple steps to make complex designs. I made the same mistake in the bridge project, where we confidently predicted ~800 Newtons, but it got crushed at around 300 Newtons, because our bridge hasn't been tested with prelimiary prototypes. Complex designs that haven't been tested in real world are generally not going to work. So, going through more and smaller (i.e., simpler) iterations of building/validating may be helpful. AI is great at making complex designs that don't work -- my future self will have to steer the AI to work in smaller chunks, and only move on to the next step when I understand / can verify that the code works. Slow down!
