Introduction

The purpose of this portfolio is to present my engineering work up to date in relation to my current position on engineering design, and to examine the effectiveness of Praxis design concepts, tools, models, and frameworks (CTMFs) I have used.

In my first year of Engineering Science, I realized that all stages of engineering design require a balance between complexity and simplicity; moreover, complexity emerges from a buildup of simple steps. This realization was formed by my experiences with Praxis I, CIV 102, Praxis II, and design team projects. The CTMFs that I have used also helped form my current position, and I will document them here for future reference.

Hoover Dam Model -- the Model that Frames My Position

Here, I will introduce the CTMF that frames my position; I will expand upon my position in the position subpage.

The Hoover Dam Model represents the relationship between reality and theory in an engineering context. We model the world as it is, which is complex, into the world as we perceive it, which is simplified. In modelling, we necessarily simplify the complex world we live in. Bias and experience may influence what we choose to simplify, and what not to. Then, using theory and prototyping, we represent the world as we dream it. For example, we would make a schematic of our design based on a map of the region. The world as we dream it is then built into the world as we create it. In building, the design faces complex reality once again, so the simplifications we have made when modelling may prove to be problematic during implementation. We can reduce the harm of simplifications by verifying and validating our design well.

FDCR -- the Model for Praxis Engineering Design Processes

Praxis splits the engineering design process into four strands; in a real design process, the strands would be intertwined.

  1. Frame: Perceiving and defining the engineering opportunity with the help of Need, Goals and Objectives (NGOs).
  2. Diverge: Coming up with design candidates.
  3. Converge: Evaluating and selecting candidates based on requirements and evaluation criteria (translated from NGOs), and stakeholder feedback.
  4. Represent: Communicating the design through documentation, sketches, prototypes, and other presentations.
